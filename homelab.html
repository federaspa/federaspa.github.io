Homelab

An old desktop that took over most of what I used to pay cloud services for.

The map

Run an everyday job and watch it travel through the setup, or switch off a container and see who notices. Hover over any part to see what it does.

The story

In autumn 2026 I gave my old desktop a second life: a 2014 Intel i5 with a hard drive from 2008 and 8 GB of RAM. Today it runs Proxmox and a handful of isolated Linux containers, and it does a lot of what I used to rely on cloud services for.

It started with photos. Immich now backs up my phone, Nextcloud holds my files, Jellyfin and Navidrome stream my films and music, and Kavita serves my manga. Pi-hole blocks ads for the whole house, and Tailscale lets me reach everything when I'm away.

Then I started building my own tools on top of it: a Spotify-Wrapped-style monthly recap of my listening, and a job tracker that collects AI and ML postings every morning and has Claude score them against my CV. The server also runs itself to a degree. A Telegram bot lets me manage it from my phone, Uptime Kuma and disk-health checks raise alerts, and every Sunday Claude writes me a report on how the week went.

What it taught meClose

Hardware has opinions

The 2008 drive froze the PC at startup until I tracked it down and retired it. Since then, files live on a 4 TB NAS drive, and the old 500 GB laptop drive holds backups. I later took the RAM from 8 to 24 GB, which gave the containers room to grow.

Isolation first

Every container is unprivileged. Apps that only need to read data get read-only mounts, and nothing talks to Docker directly: a read-only proxy sits in between. Tools I don't fully trust get a container of their own, so a bad download can't reach the rest.

Write everything down

Every setup step and every fix goes into one running guide, so the server can be rebuilt from scratch and the same problem never costs me twice.
